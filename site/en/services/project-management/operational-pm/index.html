Lasting, stable systems

For us, strategic project management means evaluating projects, bringing about decisions and establishing structures that allow projects to thrive. The link between organisation and technology is a key focus here.

Selecting projects

Which projects should be carried out?

 

Every organisation that has to prioritise several projects against each other faces this question. It is important to keep long-term opportunities and the economic framework in view. Clear selection criteria help to maintain a healthy balance.

Learn more.

Standards for projects

Project management should make use of your company's knowledge of your market and your product.

 

Based on successful projects from your industry and your company, as well as from other industries and international standards, an optimal project management approach can be developed. One way is a project management handbook that guides project managers through difficult situations.

Learn more.

Optimising projects

To know how current projects can get even better, you need to know where projects stand.

 

An outside perspective can help you take the decisive step forward. Assessments, structured interviews, training, a joint task force or other measures can get your organisation in shape.

Learn more.

Making use of project results

Projects are limited in time and characterised by a high degree of dynamism.

 

After a project, the goal is to make the best possible use of the results, learn from best practices and sharpen the saw.

Learn more.

Leveraging networks

 

Strategic project management has a significant medium- to long-term impact on the success of your organisation and its project culture, and lets you look beyond day-to-day business. Whether in international cooperations, research assignments, the development of entirely new products or the reorganisation of your own company, the challenges are diverse. We will gladly support you along the way with advice and hands-on help.
