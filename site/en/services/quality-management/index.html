Quality management with blinkers on?

In-house quality programmes often rely on a single, simple and easily achievable aspect of quality and disregard everything else. They concentrate on an aspect that is almost incidental to true quality and ignore the points that really matter.

Time as a factor in quality management

Quality takes time. The first task of quality management is to ensure the quality of the schedules that have been drawn up. However, these schedules should be agreed with quality managers in advance, because only experienced quality managers can provide a sound estimate of the effort involved. The project deadlines are then based on this estimate.

That is why time plays a special role at EOS. Only when there is enough time can the desired level of quality be achieved.

Quantity versus quality

Quality and quantity are usually inversely proportional: the lower the quantity, the higher the quality.

We will be happy to discuss with you where this balance lies in your company and what the best solution is for you.

Managing resources in quality management

A quality management programme can influence strategic resources and processes to such an extent that the overall quality of the product decreases, even though quality increases in one particular area.

This is possible because of the interaction between quality management and other areas.
